The journey · 01

Six steps from private to public.

Each company takes the same staircase — only the timing and the noise around it differ. Watch each step light up as you scroll.

01

🏠Private company

Owned by founders, family, and early backers. Value is whatever the last private round agreed on.

02

📈Needs growth capital

New factories, new markets, or debt that needs clearing — beyond what founders and VCs can keep funding.

03

🏦Bankers step in

Merchant bankers value the company, write the DRHP, and build the book of institutional demand.

04

🚀IPO launches

The issue opens. Retail, HNI, and institutional investors bid within a price band over 3–5 days.

05

🧑‍💼Shares are allotted

Demand is tallied, the cut-off price is fixed, and shares are allotted — often on a lottery basis.

06

🔔Company gets listed

Shares begin trading on NSE/BSE. The company now answers to shareholders and a live stock price.

Who's in the room · 03

Every IPO has the same cast.

🏦

Merchant Banker (BRLM)

Lead manager who prices the issue, drafts the DRHP, and runs the roadshow with institutional investors.

🏛️

SEBI

The regulator that vets disclosures, approves the prospectus, and protects retail investors from misleading claims.

🧮

Registrar

Processes applications, runs the allotment lottery when oversubscribed, and handles refunds.

🏢

Anchor Investors

Large institutions allotted shares a day before the issue opens, signalling confidence to the broader market.

📰

Underwriters

Commit to buying unsold shares, guaranteeing the company gets its target capital regardless of demand.

🧑‍💻

Retail Investors

You — bidding within the price band through UPI/ASBA, hoping the lottery and the listing both go your way.

Read before you bid · 05

What an IPO doesn't promise you.

Listing gains aren't guaranteed

Grey market premium is sentiment, not a contract. Plenty of "hot" IPOs have listed below issue price.

Allotment is a lottery, not a queue

Applying early doesn't help. In an oversubscribed issue, the registrar draws lots — full stop.

Read the DRHP, not just the hype

The draft prospectus discloses use of funds, litigation, and risk factors — the parts roadshows skip.

Lock-ins create later supply

Anchor and promoter shares unlock on schedule — often pressuring price months after listing.

Speak the language · 06

IPO terms, decoded.

DRHP — Draft Red Herring Prospectus+

The document filed with SEBI containing the company's financials, risks, and business model before the issue opens. "Red herring" because the price isn't final yet.

Price band+

The range within which investors bid — e.g. ₹340–₹360. The final cut-off price is set after the book-building process closes.

Lot size+

The minimum number of shares you must apply for as one unit — set by the company so a minimum bid stays within a target rupee range.

Grey market premium (GMP)+

An unofficial, unregulated price at which IPO shares trade before listing — a sentiment gauge, not a guarantee of listing-day gains.

ASBA+

Application Supported by Blocked Amount — your bid amount is blocked, not debited, in your bank account until allotment is finalised.
